Three findings for the compiler branch came out of it, none in its register yet:

- **MR1. `TreeUnpickler.readIndexedDef` leaves its `PoisonTree` when the read unwinds.** The sentinel is set before `readNewDef()` and removed after it with no `try`/`finally`; a reader cancelled or aborted as a cycle victim inside a TASTy definition leaves it in the file's shared `treeAtAddr`, and the next reader of that address (the waiter taking over the file root, the victim's own retry, a later request: the unpickler outlives runs) fails with "Cyclic reference while unpickling definition". This is the signature of I87's unfound second source (`UnpicklingError ... address 350 in unit /Soak.scala`, trait `Integral`). The model ([`poison_waiter_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1213), [`scrub_waiter_takes_over`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1220)) and the toy (`-root-file-leak`: 573 of 900 cancel runs, 203 of 336, 7 of 48 victim pairs, all 0 with the `finally`) agree. The fix is the `finally`, the TASTy analogue of I97. The single-reader session branch is exposed too, for the life of the driver.
- **MR2. A wait-for cycle on roots is a deadlock whether or not it is a dependency cycle, so the refinement decides what to do, not whether.** Report a cycle only when every waited-on fact is on its owner's completion stack ([`depCycle_unranked`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L934), sound for every rule set); otherwise back off, which needs re-runnable Namer completions (I8) to be clean between Namer roots. Peeks that §4.1 turned into waits (`isCompleting`, `typeParams`, `awaitOtherReader`) are wait-for edges that are not dependency edges: the sequential move at such a site is the absent path, and the force on the other side must wait for it rather than report ([`peekForce_report_both_fail`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1125) is what happens otherwise: both requests fail, an answer no sequential order gives).
- **MR3. The victim rule's retry needs a backoff.** Two body-typing readers over two coarse roots in opposite orders abort each other for ever under a fair schedule that never lapses into alternation ([`livelock_forever`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L344)); wound-wait by age does not help without a wait queue ([`byAge_livelock_forever`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L382)); a retry that waits until the collided-with party holds nothing finishes every schedule of the two- and three-party scenarios ([`backoff_progress`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L398)). The toy shows the same: the retry re-queued through the driver finishes in one abort per pair, re-run in place it hits the bound in every pair.

Every Lean module here is Mathlib-free, has no `sorry`, no `axiom` and no `native_decide` outside `example`s; the toy's results file is regenerated by `run.sh --concurrent` and its five existing scenarios are unchanged under the three existing kernels.


## 1. Roots coarser than facts, and the peek-tolerated cycle (`Query/Roots.lean`)

### Summary for a busy reader

The machine of `Query/Concurrent.lean` is generalised by a `root : Q → R`: a hole per fact as before (the symbol's `InProgress`), plus a claim per root (`CompletionRoots`), reentrant for its owner. Linearizability does not care (`run_agree`, same invariant, same proof shape). What breaks is the old theorem that a wait-for cycle is a dependency cycle: with two facts per root, ranked rules whose sequential evaluation succeeds in both orders still let a schedule close a wait-for cycle on roots, and the §4.2 rule reports a false `CyclicReference` ([`lock_order_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1039), I54 as a theorem). Waiting through it instead is a deadlock ([`lock_order_deadlock`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1055)). So the question is never *whether* to act on a wait-for cycle but *what* to do: report, or make someone yield.

The refinement answers it. A wait-for edge is a *dependency edge* when the fact the waiter wants is on the owner's stack of frames, not merely in a root the owner holds. Under a ranked fixed point a cycle of dependency edges is impossible at every reachable state ([`no_dep_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L469), [`cycle_impossible`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L455)), whatever the roots; so reporting `CyclicReference` only on a dependency cycle is sound ([`depCycle_unranked`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L934)), and a lock-order cycle is broken by backing a party off, after which every schedule gives the sequential answers ([`lock_order_refine_ok`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1063)). With injective roots every wait-for edge is a dependency edge and the old `waitCycle_unranked` is recovered under every policy.

The peek hypothesis for I68 is confirmed on one shape and corrected on the other. Two facts that peek each other ([`Interrupt.peekBoth`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L731)) are benign sequentially in both orders, and under §4.1 (a peek at another reader's completion waits) two readers entering from opposite sides close a wait-for cycle on *facts*, which the §4.2 rule reports ([`peekBoth_report_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1174)): a cycle no sequential order has. The force-peek pair (`a` forces `b`, `b` peeks `a`) is benign in one order only; its surprise is that §4.2 can make *both* demands fail, an answer neither order gives ([`peekForce_report_both_fail`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1125)). The remedy, a peek that takes the absent path when its wait would close a wait-for cycle, and a force that waits for a peek on the other side to do so, gives one of the two sequential answers on every schedule for both shapes ([`peekForce_yield_sequential`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1149), [`peekBoth_yield_sequential`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1188)), never a third. [`Concurrent.peek_own_agrees`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L950) could not see any of this: it assumed ranked rules, and both shapes are cycles when the peek is read as a force.

Against dotc, the best-supported hypothesis for "Cyclic reference involving class Setting" is this peek-tolerated shape between the *class* `Setting` and the type alias `SettingDependencies = List[(Setting[?], Any)]` that one of its constructor parameters mentions: the class completion forces the alias, the alias completion asks the class for its `typeParams`, which is a peek sequentially and a wait on the class root with parallel readers (`ClassDenotation.typeParams`, I73/I75). Evidence and alternatives in §4 below; it is a hypothesis, not a confirmed trace.

One more thing a coarse root carries: scratch. `TreeUnpickler.readIndexedDef` leaves a `PoisonTree` in the file's map if the reader unwinds mid-definition; the waiter that takes the root over reports a false "Cyclic reference while unpickling" ([`poison_waiter_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1213)); clearing it on unwinding hands the waiter a clean root ([`scrub_waiter_takes_over`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1220)). The ranked theorems need that scrub as a hypothesis, which is itself the finding.

### 1. The machine

[`RSt`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L75) is [`Concurrent.CSt`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L76) (store, per-fact holes with owner, two forcers) plus `claims : R → Option Who` and `poison : Q → Bool`. A `Cfg` holds `root`, the [`PeekMode`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L61) of Track I, a [`CyclePolicy`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L58) (`report` = the design's §4.2 and the old machine; `wait` = never act; `refine` = report a dependency cycle, back off otherwise), `yields` (the peek remedy), `scrub` (an unwinding owner clears its scratch) and `absent`.

`want` follows `completeUnderRootClaim`: a finished fact is read; a hole of one's own is a cycle; a hole of the other's (`InProgress` not `ownedHere`, `awaitOthers`) or a root claimed by the other (`acquire`) is a wait, unless `closesCycle`; a root of one's own is reentrant (`c.holds += 1`); else the root is claimed and the rule entered. `closesCycle` is `cycleChain` with two claimants: is the other forcer blocked (`blockedOn`: its question's fact unfinished and its root claimed by me). `isDepCycle` is the refinement: my wanted fact on the other's stack *and* its wanted fact on mine. `publish` releases the hole and, when no frame of the forcer is left in the root, the claim. `backoff` drops frames and releases holes and claims but keeps the demand (the aborted victim's request is retried). The yielding peek lives in `step`: a peek at a hole or root of the other's whose wait would close a cycle takes the absent path; symmetrically `onCycle` makes a *force* whose wait would close a cycle through the other's *peek* wait rather than report, because the peek will yield at its next step.

Progress is checked by `decide` on the scenarios, as in Track I; the theorems are about what is stored and reported.

### 3. What the model says for dotc

**The refinement is the I68 fix, and it is a two-part rule.** `PerKeyClaims.acquire` today asks one question of a wait-for chain (does it exist) and answers it with victim selection. The model splits the question. *Whether* to act: always, because a wait-for cycle on roots is a deadlock whether or not it is a dependency cycle ([`lock_order_deadlock`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1055)); a bounded wait (`coarseCycleWaitNanos`) is a way of acting late. *What* to do: report `CyclicReference` only if every edge of the chain is a dependency edge, meaning the claim each party waits on protects a fact that is on its owner's stack of completions in progress, not merely in the root it holds (`isDepCycle`); otherwise one party backs off (abort and retry, or the waiter re-looks after a release) and nobody reports. The soundness side is a theorem for every rule set ([`depCycle_unranked`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L934)); the completeness side, that a lock-order cycle is then resolved, is [`lock_order_refine_ok`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1063) on the scenario. In dotc's terms the test "does the waited-on completion's rule ask for the waiter's symbol" of §11 is exactly `isDepCycle`, read off the owner's stack: `Reader` would carry the list of denotations it is completing (it already counts `namerCompletionDepth`), and `cycleChain` would check, per edge, that the claim's `What` subject is in the owner's list. For a Namer root (one symbol) the check is trivially true; for a TASTy file, a bring-forward or an inherited cache it is where the lock orders are told apart.

**Peeks that became waits are the second source of false cycles.** §4.1 turned thirteen `isCompleting` sites, and `ClassDenotation.typeParams` (I73/I75), `awaitOtherReader` in `maybeParamAccessors` and `setTrackedConstrParam`, into waits on the other reader's root. The model says a wait at a peek site is a wait-for edge that is *not* a dependency edge in the sequential semantics: sequentially the peek would have taken the absent path on its own hole. When such an edge closes a cycle, the right move is the sequential one, the absent path (`yields`), and the force on the other side must not report but wait for it (`onCycle`'s first branch): otherwise the force reports, the peek enters the freed fact and meets its own hole, and both requests fail ([`peekForce_report_both_fail`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1125)). This is cheap to implement at `cyclicWait`: when the party that would report is at a peek (`CompletionLock.peek` is already the `What` of those waits), return "in progress" to the caller instead of throwing; when the party is at a force and the other party's claim is a `peek` wait, keep waiting.

**A coarse root's scratch needs rule 4 too.** `readIndexedDef` (`TreeUnpickler.scala:953-965`) writes `treeAtAddr()(start) = PoisonTree` and removes it after `readNewDef()` with no `try`/`finally`. An owner aborted as a cycle victim or cancelled inside the definition leaves the sentinel; the waiter that takes the file root over (`awaitOthers` then `acquire`) reads it as "Cyclic reference while unpickling definition at address …" ([`poison_waiter_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1213)), the I87 soak's unfound second source has this signature. The `finally` is the fix ([`scrub_waiter_takes_over`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1220)), and [`PoisonInv`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L417) in the model says why it is needed for every other theorem: without it `run_ranked_no_cyc` does not hold.

**Linearizability needs none of this.** `run_agree` holds for every policy and every root function, so the coarse barrier is as safe as the fine one; what coarseness costs is progress and the truth of the cycle report, never the store.

The hypothesis, then: row 2 is the cycle. Reader R1 completes `class Setting` (root: the class) and, indexing its constructor, forces the alias `SettingDependencies` (root: the alias). Reader R2 completes the alias (or a member of `SettingGroup` such as `StringSetting`, `Settings.scala:462`, whose parameter `depends: SettingDependencies` forces the alias first), and the alias's right-hand side `List[(Setting[?], Any)]` asks `Setting.typeParams`; the class is `InProgress` on R1, so R2 waits on the class root. R1's wait on the alias root now closes a chain; both parties are inside Namer completions, every root is a symbol, so after two looks `cyclicWait` throws `CyclicReference(Setting)` in one of them: "Cyclic reference involving class Setting". Neither sequential order has this cycle: from the class, `typeParams` is read from R1's own `TempClassInfo`; from the alias, `typeParams` of a class not in progress is computed without completing it. The model's [`peekForce_report_both_fail`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1125) adds that, depending on which reader's look comes second, *both* completions can fail, which would explain "what follows from `Setting` losing its members" in I68's divergent files. The members-in-opposite-orders reading of §11 is consistent with this (any two of `Setting`'s members whose completions reach the class through row 2 or row 1 from different readers), but the alias is the one edge found whose sequential form is a peek rather than a force. Rows 5 and 6 are where lock-order chains through coarse roots (inherited caches) would come from, if the trace shows a `baseClasses` or `memberNames` claim in the chain.

What would confirm or refute it: the claim log at a raise (`graphNow`) showing `class Setting` held by a reader whose `waitingOn` is `SettingDependencies` (or a `SettingGroup` member) and vice versa; or the test below.

### 5. Tests projected onto `CompletionConcurrentSuite`

Both scenarios want two readers started on two targets with the completion order forced by the targets, the single-reader run as the oracle, and `-Ddotc.interactive.failOnCycle` so that the raise is an assertion at `cyclicWait`.

**A lock-order cycle** ([`lock_order_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1039)): two roots, two facts each, cross dependencies. With Namer roots being symbols, a coarse root is needed; the inherited cache is the cheapest. Source shape:

```scala
trait Base1 { def m1: Int = 1 }
trait Base2 { def m2: Int = 2 }
class A extends Base1 { def a1 = (new B).b2; def a2 = m1 }   // a1, a2 inferred: `inherited` takes A's baseClasses cache
class B extends Base2 { def b1 = (new A).a2; def b2 = m2 }   // b1, b2 likewise
```

Reader 1 hovers `a1`, reader 2 hovers `b1`, with a chaos point after each has taken its owner's inherited-cache root and before it forces the cross member. Oracle: `a1: Int`, `b1: Int`, no diagnostics, in both single-reader orders. Failure signature under the current rule: a `CyclicReference` naming `a2` or `b2` in one reader (false), or a victim abort and retry; under the refinement, neither.

**A peek-tolerated cycle** ([`peekBoth_report_false_cycle`](../../query/Query/Roots.lean#L1174), `peekForce_*`): the `Setting` shape itself, minimised:

```scala
object S:
  type Deps = List[(Box[?], Any)]
  case class Box[T](value: T, deps: Deps = Nil)
  def mk(d: Deps): Box[Int] = Box(1, d)
```

Reader 1 completes `Box` (hover on `Box`'s name, or a request whose target is inside `Box`), reader 2 completes `mk` (whose signature forces `Deps`, whose right-hand side asks `Box.typeParams`), with a chaos point in `completeUnderRootClaim` between the root acquisition and `lockAndComplete0` for both. Oracle: both single-reader orders complete `Box`, `Deps` and `mk` without a diagnostic ([`forcePeek_memoizes_order`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L739) says the stored values may differ by order for a mutual peek; here they do not, since the alias does not depend on the class's completed info). Failure signature under the current rule: "Cyclic reference involving class Box" from `cyclicWait`, or a deadlock to the 10 s coarse timeout if a coarse root is in the chain. Under the remedy (`typeParams` on another reader's `InProgress` whose wait would close a chain takes the uncompleted-class path, `completerTypeParams`), both readers answer, and the answer equals the single-reader one.

The same test with the second reader on `Box.copy$default$2` instead of `mk` exercises row 4 (the default getter forces `copy`, whose signature mentions `Box[T]` and `Deps`) and tells whether the cycle needs the alias or any member suffices.

### 6. Scope and what the model does not cover

Two forcers only, so `cycleChain`'s walk is one edge and the victim rule (who backs off, the bounded wait, the two looks of I87) is not modelled; another agent does that. `Inj root` stands in for `root = id` so that the recovered theorems are about any renaming of facts. Progress of the back-off is checked by `decide` on the scenarios, not proved. The poison model has one sentinel per fact and treats "found poisoned with no hole" as the false cycle; `readIndexedDef`'s map is per address, which is finer than a fact and coarser than nothing, and the model does not distinguish them.

## 2. The wait-for graph read one edge at a time (`Query/WaitGraph.lean`)

### Summary for a busy reader

`cycleChain` reads the wait-for graph from fields other threads change, one edge at a time, with no snapshot. The I87 fix (stop at a counted-down claim) and the I98 fix (return the chain only if every claim in it is still up) are both checks of *latches*, and a latch that is up says "the owner has not counted down", not "the owner still waits". The model finds:

1. **I87 and I98 reconstruct** as histories in which the walk returns a chain and no instant has a real cycle ([`i87_false_chain`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L476), [`i98_false_chain`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L490)). The I98 final check subsumes the I87 entry check ([`entry_check_subsumed`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L541)): a latch counts down once, so a claim down when pushed is down at the end. The entry check only ends a walk sooner.
2. **With both checks a false chain is still possible, and every one of them goes through a party that left its wait by throwing.** [`release_window_false_chain`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L575): `A` holds claim 1 and waits on `B`'s claim 2; the walker reads claim 1 up and `A.waitingOn = 2`; `A`'s `await` returns, `A` throws out of `acquire` (an abort as the victim of another cycle, a `cycle()` raise, an interrupt in `await`), its `finally` clears `waitingOn`, it runs `claims.remove` and has not yet run `countDown`; `B` now waits on the walker's claim; the walker reads claim 2 up, `B.waitingOn = 0`, claim 0 (its own), and the final check finds 0, 2, 1 all up. `A`'s wait ended before `B`'s began: the chain joins two waits that never coexisted, exactly I98's shape, one claim further along. On the enumerated spaces (every placement of the walker's eight reads among the other claimants' steps: 12870, 3003 and 12870 histories by `decide`, 180180 + 43758 by `native_decide`) every false chain has an `unwind` in its history ([`false_chain_needs_unwind`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L660)), and without one both checks are sound ([`no_unwind_sound`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L648), [`release_sound`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L653)): releases, woken waiters, keys taken over by new claims during the walk never make a false chain. The I98 argument is right about claims and wrong about waits; it is sound exactly when no party of the chain throws during the walk.
3. **The release window (`remove` then `countDown`) is one way the throwing party's claim looks held; the window between the throw and the release is another and wider.** Counting down before removing, or asking the registry (`claims.get(c.key) eq c`), closes the first ([`cd_first_closes_window`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L587), [`map_check_closes_window`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L591)) and not the second ([`cd_first_not_enough`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L608): `histUnwinding`, the same history with `A` still unwinding, claim 1 registered and up). Between the throw inside the inner `acquire` and the `locked` frame that releases claim 1 run the completion's own `finally` blocks (`Touched` reset, the `UnwoundCompletion` guard, `nestedTyper` bookkeeping), so that window is microseconds, not two statements.
4. **Two corrections are sound on the spaces.** Re-reading the edges in the final check (`waiter.waitingOn eq claim` for each edge, with count-down-first so that "up" implies "registered"): [`edge_check_sound`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L679). A global sequence number bumped by every acquire, grant, wait, wake and release step, read before the first edge and compared after the final check (the seqlock shape): [`snapshot_sound`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L685), and the chain is a cycle at the instant it is returned, not only at some instant. The edge re-check can return a chain that was real and is being resolved (`transient`), which the abortable-party rule and the two-looks rule absorb; the snapshot cannot.
5. **The two-looks rule is sound on the space** ([`two_looks_real`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L744), and the 1081575-history version by `native_decide`): two consecutive looks returning identical chains imply a cycle real at some instant. The reason: the second look re-reads every edge, so every party is inside `acquire` during it; a party that threw never waits again (its claim in the chain is the same object, so it would have to be the same wait); so nobody threw during the first look, and its waits all stood at its end. A false chain cannot repeat, whatever a party re-waits on each slice. What the rule costs is only that the window of item 3 must be shorter than a slice (5 ms), which under 8–10 parallel JVMs is not a given.
6. **The first-look abort.** A claimant that `canAbort` aborts on the chain of its first look, so the false chain of item 2 cancels and restarts a body-typing reader for nothing ([`first_look_false_abort`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L759)). It needs a party of the chain to throw during the walk, so its rate is bounded by the rate of unwinds (cycle victims, cancellations); cheap, and removed by either correction of item 4.

**On the unfound second source of false cycles (I87's residue, the `UnpicklingError: Cyclic reference while unpickling definition`).** The model's residue (item 2) raises a `CyclicReference` only through the two-looks rule, which item 5 shows is sound unless a party stays between its throw and its `countDown` for a whole slice. The likelier source is not in the graph walk: `TreeUnpickler.readIndexedDef` (scratch `pr84/TreeUnpickler.scala` lines 953–965) sets `treeAtAddr()(start) = PoisonTree` before `readNewDef()` and removes it after, with no `try`/`finally`. A reader that unwinds inside a TASTy completion (aborted as a wait-for cycle victim, a Metals cancel, a writer's sticky cancellation) leaves the poison in the file's shared address map, and the next reader to take the file root over asserts "Cyclic reference while unpickling definition" at that address (trait `Integral`, address 350, is a classpath symbol). That is a shape-2 sentinel in root state not reset on unwind, the TASTy analogue of I97 (`LazyRef.computed`). Test: force a classpath TASTy symbol whose completion forces a source-file Namer completion, cancel at that checkpoint (the `util.Chaos` hook), then force the same symbol from another reader; the fix is a `try`/`finally` around `readNewDef()` (or a poison that records its thread, so a reader meeting another thread's poison waits or retries rather than asserting).

### 1. The model

A state (`St`) is every claim object ever made (identity = index, so a key acquired again gets a new claim and a new latch), the registry (`reg`, the `ConcurrentHashMap`), the latches that have counted down (`down`), each claimant's `waitingOn`, the key each claimant is inside `acquire` of and has not been granted (`acquiring`, the ground truth of "waits for"), the claimants that have thrown out of an `acquire` (`unwound`), and an epoch.

Events of the claimants other than the walker: `start w k` (enters `acquire k`, finds it held), `wait w` (`me.waitingOn = c` before `await`), `wake w` (`await` returned, by timeout or count-down, and the `finally` cleared `waitingOn`), `grant w` (the key is free, `putIfAbsent` a new claim), `unwind w` (throws out of `acquire`, `waitingOn` already cleared), `remove c` and `countDown c` (the two statements of `release`, in the order the design says; between them the owner does nothing else). Each is enabled only where the real code can take it (`bg` returns `none` otherwise; a space is filtered to admitted histories). The walker's events are `read` (its next read of `cycleChain`, in the Scala order: `c.done.getCount`, push, `c.owner eq me`, `c.owner.waitingOn`, and at the end one `getCount` per claim of the chain in the order the list was built) and `look` (its `await` returned; it re-reads the registry and walks again).

Designs (`Design`): the checks `i87`, `i98`; `cdFirst` (release counts down first); `mapCheck` (final check asks the registry); `edgeCheck` (final check re-reads each edge); `snapshot` (epoch unchanged since the first read).

Ground truth: `realCycle s me` follows `acquiring` and `reg` from the walker and asks whether it comes back. It says nothing about `waitingOn` or latches: it is the deadlock. A chain is *false* ([`Run.falseChain`](../../query/Query/WaitGraph.lean#L430)) if it was returned and no state up to the instant of its return has a real cycle; *transient* if a real cycle existed at some instant before and not at the return.

Claimants `W = 0` (the walker, holds key 0, inside `acquire 1`), `A = 1` (holds key 1), `B = 2` (holds key 2); claim `i` is claimant `i`'s initial claim.

### 3. What the model leaves out

One claimant per thread (a claimant that moves between threads breaks "`waitingOn` non-null means inside `acquire`"). Re-entrance (`holds`), which never waits. The claimant's own decision on a chain (abort, wait for an abortable party, two looks) is not an event: `unwind` stands for any way out of `acquire` that throws, and in the real system those are: an abort at the claimant's own look (a real cycle, by item 2 applied inductively to the first chain of a history, so the first victim is right and the walker's chain through it is a stale view of a cycle being resolved: a double victim, not a false cycle in the strict sense), a `cycle()` raise (same), and an interrupt in `await` (strictly false; dotc's interactive cancellation is polled, so this needs `Thread.interrupt`, which Metals' presentation compiler does not use on the compiler threads as far as the sources read show). The 1000-step bound (a walk revisiting a claim ends with null). Time: a history that keeps a party in its window for a whole slice is admitted; the note weighs that above.

A reading the model sharpens: I98's "it is not I87's unfound second source: inside Namer completions the second look, a slice later, already finds the released claim and drops the chain" is right for the latch window and right for the two-looks rule (item 5), so the residue of the graph walk is false *aborts* of body-typing readers, not false *raises*.

### 4. Tests for the real system

**Lincheck, `PerKeyClaims` with stub claimants** (the shape of `CompletionRootsLincheckTest` and `SharingOnCompletionRootsLincheckTest.claim`, stress mode; the waits are timed, so model checking does not apply):

1. *The release window.* Three threads, keys `k0 k1 k2`. T0: `locked(k0) { locked(k1) {} }`. T2: `locked(k2) { locked(k0) {} }`. Sequential expectation: no thread but a real cycle victim is ever `Cancelled`; with `k2`'s holder T2 waiting on `k0` only after T1 has left `k1`, T0 must never abort. Check: count aborts per claimant against the real cycles (a `CycleHandler` that records `graphNow` at each raise); any abort of a claimant whose `graphNow` at the abort shows a party not waiting is the false chain. Expected: fails on #84 (T0 aborted for nothing), passes with the edge re-check or the snapshot.
2. *The throw-to-release window.* As 1 but the hook pauses T1 after `Cancelled` is thrown and before `release` runs (a `finally` in the test's claimed body that sleeps 1 ms on cancellation). Expected: fails on #84 and on count-down-first; passes with the edge re-check or the snapshot.
3. *Soundness of the first look under churn.* Four threads doing `locked(a) { locked(b) {} }` in the same order `a < b` over three keys, so no real cycle is possible, with one thread per round aborting itself (a test-only `Cancelled` from inside the inner claimed body, so claims unwind through `locked`'s `finally`). Invariant: `aborted` of every claimant is zero except the self-aborter's. 4. *The two-looks rule.* As 1 with no claimant `canAbort` and `coarseCycleWaitNanos = 0`, and the pause set to 10 ms (longer than a slice): a `cycle()` raise with the pause is the two-looks rule meeting the window of item 3. Expected: raises on #84 (rarely without the pause, deterministically with it); none with the corrections.

**JCStress** (`@JCStressTest`, `@Actor` × 3, `@Arbiter`): the arbiter records `(aborted0, aborted1, aborted2, raised)` and the acceptable outcomes are those with at most one victim per real cycle; the actors are the three bodies of test 1 with `canAbort` on T1 only. JCStress's millions of iterations find the two-statement window where Lincheck's hundreds do not.

**`graphNow` as a post-mortem.** Add an `AtomicLong seq` to `PerKeyClaims`, bumped at `putIfAbsent` success, at `release`'s `remove`, and at `countDown`; stamp each `Claim` with `acquiredAt` and `removedAt`/`downAt` (volatile longs), and each `Claimant` with `waitingSince` (the `seq` read before `waitingOn = c`). Then a raised cycle's chain is real at some instant iff there is a `seq` value `t` with `acquiredAt ≤ t` for every claim, `removedAt > t` (or unset) for every claim, and `waitingSince ≤ t` for every party and the party still waiting at `t`; print the chain with those stamps at the raise, and the post-mortem is a sort. The same `seq` is the snapshot of item 4 for free: `cycleChain` reads it first and last.

**Patch, in order of preference.** (a) The snapshot: `val s0 = seq.get` before the loop; after the all-held check, `if seq.get != s0 then null` (the claimant retries at its next slice, and the walk is short enough that it usually completes in one epoch; under contention it degrades to the two-looks rule's delay, never to a false chain). (b) Count down before remove, plus the edge re-check in the final loop (the chain kept as pairs of claim and waiter); the woken waiter then spins for the two statements between `countDown` and `remove`, finding `getCount == 0` on a registered claim. (c) At least: make the final check ask the registry (`Claim` gets its key), which closes the `remove`/`countDown` window the task asked about but not the throw-to-release one.

## 3. The victim rule and the read/write regime (`Query/Victim.lean`, `Query/Regime.lean`)

### Summary for a busy reader

**The livelock is real, and it is the adversary's schedule, not the fair one.** Two body readers whose needs are in opposite orders abort each other for ever under the as-coded policy ([`livelock_forever`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L344): a four-step period, each party twice, maps the state to itself). Round-robin finishes them, and so does any six-step prefix followed by a round-robin tail ([`dotc_eventually_fair_progress`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L363)): the livelock needs the adversary never to lapse. That is why the test's cap of 20 aborts is reached only on a loaded machine, and why the cross-thread cycle "aborts off 19–25 times in 30 rounds, at most once per request" on a quiet one. Age (wound-wait on the detected cycle) does not fix it: without a queue the youngest victim re-takes its first key before the survivor moves ([`byAge_livelock_forever`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L382)). The backoff does: a victim retries only once the party it waited on holds nothing, and every schedule of the two- and three-party scenarios finishes ([`backoff_progress`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L398), [`backoff_progress_three`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L404)). The backoff is what a lock's wait queue gives for free; `acquire`'s retry-from-the-driver has none.

**Poison is unavoidable without re-runnable Namer completions.** The smallest case-3 scenario has three keys: two Namer signatures and one TASTy file, both readers inside a signature, the cycle through the file. Whichever bound runs out first aborts and leaves its signature an `UnwoundCompletion`, which then cancels both requests until a fresh run ([`poison_smallest`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L427), [`poison_until_fresh`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L433): I74's "4 of 20 cells"). With two keys there is no poison under any schedule ([`two_keys_no_poison`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L442)). Letting the party whose latest claim is coarse yield that claim alone, with the backoff, avoids it ([`coarseYield_no_poison`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L454)), but a scenario where every party has a fine key above its coarse one leaves no clean move: every abort poisons, and waiting is a deadlock ([`poison_unavoidable`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L479)).

**I68's lock-order cycle has no good move either, until I8.** With `isDependencyCycle` false and both readers inside Namer completions, raising is a false `CyclicReference`, aborting poisons, waiting deadlocks ([`lockOrder_no_good_move`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L519)). With re-runnable completions, abort the newest party with the backoff: every schedule finishes, nothing poisoned, nothing raised ([`lockOrder_rerunnable_progress`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L530)). And with a policy that raises only on a dependency cycle, a raised event implies the bit, for every state ([`raised_implies_depCycle`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L569)): `cyclesRaised` can be asserted against it.

**The regime's one-version property holds for every schedule, by induction, not by decide** ([`run_inv`](../../query/Query/Regime.lean#L277), [`done_one_version`](../../query/Query/Regime.lean#L294)): a reader never crosses a write because the write waits for it. It fails as soon as a reader is admitted beside the regime ([`rogue_reads_two_versions`](../../query/Query/Regime.lean#L445), I50). Write latency is the longest remaining distance to a checkpoint among the running readers ([`reader_leaves_within`](../../query/Query/Regime.lean#L321), general; [`latency_is_longest_gap`](../../query/Query/Regime.lean#L356), gaps 2 and 7). ### Verdicts

**Livelock.** Possible, with the minimal scenario and schedule above. The design's "in principle" is exact: it needs an adversarial scheduler that never lapses into alternation, so a soak will show it under load and not on a quiet machine, and a cap of 20 aborts is a detector, not a fix. The fix is the backoff: the aborted reader's retry waits for the holder it collided with to release (in dotc: `Reader.abort` records the claim's owner; `reading`'s retry loop waits on that claimant's "holds nothing" before re-entering `body`). Age ordering alone does not help, because `acquire` has no wait queue: the victim retries from the driver and wins the race for its first key.

**I74.** Case 3's bounded wait is a timer that ends in poison whenever both parties are inside a Namer completion, and in the smallest such scenario it poisons a signature both requests need, so both are cancelled until a fresh run: the "4 of 20 cells" is the model's [`poison_until_fresh`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L433). A policy that makes the coarse-claim holder yield only its coarse claim (a TASTy completion is re-runnable) with the backoff removes the poison when the coarse claim is the party's latest; when a Namer completion sits above it, no victim policy avoids the poison. So I8 (re-runnable Namer completions) is the fix, and until then the yield-the-coarse-claim rule is the best available: fewer poisons, never more.

**I68.** With `isDependencyCycle` false the as-coded policy's only outcomes are a false error, a poison, or a deadlock. The right rule is: raise only when `isDependencyCycle`; otherwise, with I8, abort the newest party (ages kept across retries, with the backoff). Without I8 there is no clean move, and waiting is a true deadlock ([`lockOrder_wait_stuck`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L510)), so the choice is between the false error, which is cached as the symbol's info for the session, and the poison, which a fresh run heals; the model's verdict is the poison, since the design already prices a cycle at one symbol for one request.

### Testing ideas, projected onto dotc

- **`CompletionConcurrentSuite`, a livelock test.** Two readers, two coarse roots (two TASTy files or two bring-forwards) needed in opposite orders, with a chaos point (`util.Chaos.at`) after each reader's abort and before its retry that lets the test choose who runs: the schedule `[A, A, B, B]` repeated. Under the as-coded policy `waitCyclesAborted` passes 20 on both; under the backoff it stays at 1 and both requests answer. The test is the model's [`livelock_forever`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L344) and [`backoff_progress`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L398) made into one scenario.
- **A chaos test for the poison path.** Three roots: two Namer signatures in the open file and one classpath TASTy root; reader A completes signature 1 whose body types a classpath member (needs the file); reader B is inside signature 2 reading the file and forces signature 1. Set `coarseCycleWaitNanos` to a millisecond. Expect: an `UnwoundCompletion` on one signature, both requests cancelled, and the next fresh run clean. Then the same with the yield-the-coarse-claim rule: no `UnwoundCompletion`. `CompletionRootsLincheckTest` with stub readers can run the claims-level version directly: three keys, two claimants, the stub's `canAbort` false while it holds a fine key.
- **`cyclesRaised` only when `isDependencyCycle`.** In `acquire`'s case 4, assert (or count separately) that `cycle()` is called only when the dependency check holds; the model's [`raised_implies_depCycle`](../../query/Query/Victim.lean#L569) is the invariant. A unit test on `PerKeyClaims` with two stub claimants in opposite orders over two fine keys and a `CycleHandler` that records the bit: the as-coded policy raises on a lock order, the fixed one does not.
- **Regime: one version per request.** A reader that records `ctx.runId` at every checkpoint and asserts it never changes within one `reading` body; run it beside a writer loop. Then the I50 shape: a second executor calling `runUpTo0` outside `reading`, which must trip the assertion (the stock bug), and must not once it goes through `reading`.
- **Write latency.** A reader in an implicit search with no checkpoint for N trees beside a writer: the writer's wait is that reader's remaining search, which argues for the checkpoint in implicit search (§9's open question).

### Where the model abstracts

A party's kind is read off its stack (inside a Namer completion when it holds a fine key), so `namerCompletionDepth` is the count of fine keys held; a Namer completion the request started inside, outside any modelled claim, would be a fine key held from the start. Aborts and releases are atomic (a victim unwinds in one step); dotc's unwinding runs `finally` blocks over time, which only widens the windows the model finds. The bounded wait counts looks, not nanoseconds. The two-looks rule is kept (a reporter raises at its second look at the same chain) but the chains are read from a consistent state, so I87's and I98's false chains are outside this model. The regime model counts steps, so "latency" is in reader steps; and a cancelled reader is retried only by an explicit `admit`, which stands for its caller's retry.

## 4. The publication idioms over a weak memory (`Query/Publication.lean`)

### Summary

The three `dotty.tools.sharing` idioms of [retronym/scala3#84](https://github.com/retronym/scala3/issues/84) (`Stamped`, `Published`/`Payload`, `Once`) and the bugs they replace (I36, I60, I67, I78) are modelled as two-thread programs over a store-buffer memory and checked on every execution. Every claim the design's §4.4.1 makes about the pair held in a `Stamped` holder is a theorem: a reader sees a whole pair, two writers cannot be mixed, a reader at period 1 and a writer at period 2 are never confused, no fences and no argument about periods. Every bug is a counterexample: the two-field stamp/value cache is torn under store reordering in either order, and in dotc's `computeDenot` order (stamp first) it is torn under TSO too; a payload read before the acquire and a payload store after the release are torn in every memory model, so they are program-order bugs the `Payload` types rightly reject at compile time.

Two things the model adds to the design's text:

1. **The "no fences" claim is about the pair, not about the inputs of `compute`.** A `cachedWhen` cache invalidated by a store of `Stamped.Empty` loses the invalidation to a racing refill in every memory model: the refill read `Empty` and the old input, the invalidator wrote the new input and cleared, the refill stored `Stamped(current, old)`. The stamp cannot tell, because both are in one period ([`clear_loses_invalidation`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L534)). An invalidation that bumps a stamp the reader took before reading its inputs is kept ([`bump_keeps_invalidation`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L549)); the same refill with the stamp taken after the inputs is torn again ([`stamp_after_inputs_torn`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L558)). My reading of `ClassDenotation.baseTypeCache` (please verify): it is safe against this today only because its `compute` is an *empty* `EqHashMap`, filled in place afterwards, so an entry computed from stale info lands in a map the invalidation has already unlinked. Any `cachedWhen` whose `compute` produces the whole value from inputs the invalidator changes has the window. The concurrent `InheritedCacheImpl` already uses `base.structureEpoch` as a stamp; `baseTypeCache` could stamp with it rather than clear.
2. **Which test harness can find each race** is now a question with an exact answer per theorem (table below): the two-field cache in value-first order is correct under TSO by the hardware alone, so Lincheck stress on x86 cannot find it, and Lincheck's model checker, which assumes sequential consistency, cannot either; neither can find I94's shape. The design's sentence "a stamp and a value updated in place must fail `StampedLincheckTest`" is true only if the test runs on ARM, or the fields are stored in the wrong order.

### The model

- Memory: a function from a few locations to values. A value is `null`, a number, or a `holder s v`, the reference to an immutable `Stamped(s, v)`. The holder is a single value by construction: the model's one assumption beyond the hardware is JLS §17.5, that constructing a final-field object and publishing its reference is one event, so a reader that sees the reference sees the fields as constructed.
- Each thread has a buffer of plain stores (oldest first), registers, and a *view*: the value it last saw at each location. A plain store joins the buffer. A buffered store drains to memory one step at a time: under `Arch.weak` any store with no older store to its own location (store-store reordering with per-location coherence, as on Apple silicon); under `Arch.tso` the oldest (x86). A plain load returns the thread's own newest buffered store to the location if any (store-to-load forwarding), else memory, and under `weak` optionally the view (a stale read, the effect of a load reordered before an older one); a load records what it read in the view, and a drained own store too, so a thread never reads a location older than it last saw it (coherence). `release` drains the buffer in order and stores after it; `acquire` reads fresh and refreshes the whole view.
- `finals` enumerates every maximal execution's end state (fuel `2(|pw|+|pr|)` suffices: each step runs an instruction or drains a store). Scenario sizes: 30 to 104 end states, so `decide +kernel` is instant.
- Where the model is stronger than ARM or C++11 (an acquire refreshes the whole view, not only what the release's writer had drained; a release and its drain are one step), every positive theorem still rests only on: a holder is whole, a release orders earlier stores before itself, an acquire that reads a released value sees what preceded the release. Lincheck-style completeness is not claimed: the model finds the races that exist in it, and the positive theorems are sound under those three assumptions.

### Idiom misuse risks in the library's API

- `Stamped.peek(orElse)`: reads once (`val c = field`), fine. `Stamped.Empty` is `Stamped(0, null)` cast, so `cachedWhen(isCurrent)` receives a null value and must test it, as `baseTypeCache` does; a `cached` field of a non-nullable `T` never returns `Empty.value` only because stamp 0 is never current, which `Stamp[K]` documents but does not enforce (a `Stamp(0)` given would be a hit on `Empty`).
- `Stamped` invalidation by `field = Stamped.Empty` (`invalidateBaseTypeCache`, `invalidateMemberCaches`): the lost-invalidation window above. Safe where `compute` is an empty mutable structure filled afterwards; not in general. A stamp kind that the invalidator bumps is the robust form.
- `copyCaches`: `myBaseTypeCache = Stamped(myBaseTypeCache.stamp, from.baseTypeCache)` pairs this object's stamp with another object's map: deliberate, but it is a write outside the macros with no `keep` or validity test; worth a comment or a `Stamped.withValue` helper.
- `Published.plain`/`setPlain`, `Payload.plain`: named and countable, good. A `setPlain` reset is a plain store that a reader may see arbitrarily late (in the model it waits in the buffer); a reader that acquires the period after a `setPlain(Nowhere)` can still read the old period and the old payloads, consistently, so it is safe only when a stale hit is acceptable, which "a reset" implies but the docstring could say. `Payload.plain` as a hint "checked again after" is sound only if the recheck is `after(snap)`; nothing types that the recheck happens.
- `Published.Acquired` evidence outliving its block is sound (the file's [`mp_release_acquire_weak_ok`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L264) is exactly that reasoning: later loads stay ordered after the acquire), as the design says; but evidence from one acquire used for reads after a *second* writer's release still reads the first publication's payloads consistently with the first period, so a loop that re-acquires must rebind `snap`.
- `Once.peek` in `Mode.Batch` is a plain load; `Once.get`'s batch path too. The mode test is the single place where a batch base shared by accident (a `Mode.Batch` context on a reader thread) reintroduces [`once_plain_weak_torn`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L456). The design's "in Batch the accesses are plain" relies on batch meaning one thread, which nothing in the type checks.
- `Once.set`/`reset` take `Holds[K]`, so the writer is unique; good. A `Once` whose `T` has non-final fields is safe only through the release/acquire pair ([`once_release_acquire_ok`](../../query/Query/Publication.lean#L463)); a reader that reaches the object by another path (a plain field holding the same reference) is the DCL bug again.

## 5. An edit inside a body keeps the run (`ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean`)

### Summary for a busy reader

§12's rule, "an edit inside the right-hand side of one member with a declared result type keeps the run, every other statement's typed tree and every symbol, and re-indexes the member with its old symbols as a skipped statement", was stated in the tree model on one example ([`tier1_body_edit`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyTree.lean#L461)). It is now a theorem for every program of the toy language and every single-member body edit, by a general proof rather than `decide`. The proof is an erasure: `erase p` deletes the body of every member with a declared type, and [`rulesOf_erase`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L197) shows by induction over the namer's functions that the rule of every fact of every program is the rule of the erased program. So no symbol-table fact reads a declared member's body, and two programs that differ only in such a body have the same symbol table. Lifted to the tree ([`bodyEdit_rules_eq`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L434)), every fact other than the edited node's typed form has the same rule after the edit, the same `Task`, so the same value and the same trace under every oracle; that is exactly the `Same` hypothesis of [`Query.Revision.reuse_sound`](../../query/Query/Revision.lean#L235), discharged for every program ([`bodyEdit_same`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L457)), and the kept run is sound ([`bodyEdit_kept_run_sound`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L475)): reusing every entry but the edited node's returns the new fixed point, and the edited member's typed form is the only fact to recompute, when a request asks for it. Diagnostics in the toy are the error values of facts, keyed by the fact that produced them (§12's provenance), so "diagnostics outside m unchanged" is the same theorem; `keptRunOk` checks it as a multiset on a space of 1,695 edits over the 7 kernel-scenario, 5 tier and 56 two-declaration [`ToySpace.menu`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToySpace.lean#L19) programs, with the tiers by `decide +kernel` and the whole space compiled.

The catalogue: an inferred result type and an `inline` member are unsound and both are refused by `BodyEdit.ineligible`; the `inline` case is a model with a caller that splices the inlinee's body (I28), where no namer fact changes but a sibling's typed form does, which is why the exclusion is necessary and not just prudent. A template body's export, import or header is not a right-hand side (not a `ValOrDefDef`, so `editedMember` never returns it) and changes a member set. A local implicit or a lookup in another member cannot read the body (special cases of the erasure). The features the toy lacks are argued from dotc: a local child of a sealed class is guarded for the old body only (the guard inspects the typed tree), and a *new* local child in D' when a sibling has already queried `children` (a `Mirror.SumOf` summoned, exhaustivity) is a gap the mutation test should probe; default arguments are in the parameter list, outside the rhs, so they are a fresh run, and the default getters are in D's thicket and re-indexed; annotations are in `rawMods` and compared; a rhs deleted outright is classified `Body` (the comparison skips the rhs field entirely) and changes `Deferred`, which `=:=` on the info does not see, a second question for the oracle. The re-index invariant is the owner's member set being a function of the signatures ([`bodyEdit_ownNames`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L493)); the code checks it at run time (`refined.size == olds.size && news.size == olds.size`, then `=:=` on every completed info) and poisons the session otherwise.

### Claim to theorem

### The span shift and the re-index (3)

In the tree model node ids are stable and the edited member gets a fresh node; positions are paths, so there is no shift. In dotc the new content is re-parsed, every kept span is moved by `Region.span` (with the ambiguous-end rule anchored on D's span) and the old symbols are reattached: `olds` = `cls.unforcedDecls` filtered by `definesHere`, not a constructor, not an inline accessor, span inside D's old span; they are unlinked (`cls.delete`, `nestedTyper.remove`, `defTree = EmptyTree`), the copied new definition is indexed with `Typer.RefinedSymbols` = `(name, span) => olds.find(sym.name == name && sym.span.point == span.point)`, which `Namer.createOrRefine` (Namer.scala:363) uses as `prev`; then `refined.size == olds.size && news.size == olds.size`, and every completed old info `=:=` the new, else poison.

The invariant that makes this sound: the set of symbols the owner holds for D (D, its default getters, a `var`'s setter) is a function of D's signature text, and their infos are functions of the signature. In the toy: [`bodyEdit_ownNames`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L493) (the owner's member set is a function of the erased program) and [`rulesOf_erase`](../../scala-types/ScalaTypes/ToyBodyEdit.lean#L197) for the infos. What the body can add to the owner's scope: an inline accessor, made by typing a *local* inline def in D's body that reads a private member, positioned at the reference inside D (hence the `isInlineAccessor` exclusion: created by typing, not the Namer, kept as is and reused by `AccessProxies.accessorSymbol` when D' is typed; stale but harmless if D' no longer needs it). Symbols owned by D (locals, local classes, synthetic `x$n`) are invisible outside except through an inferred type, which the rule excludes; a declared type cannot name a local (`Outer#Inner` needs `Inner` a member of `Outer`; `f.type` is the declared type's singleton), so there is no path-dependent leak. The fresh-name counter continues (`x$7` for a fresh run's `x$3`): invisible to answers but to a symbol-name comparison, which the oracle normalises.

### Testing ideas projected onto dotc (4)

**`BodyEditSuite`, an oracle over single-token edits.** Corpus: the four `SessionOracleSuite` files plus hand-picked `tests/pos` files rich in the catalogue's features (sealed hierarchies with `derives`, inline methods, default arguments, exports, givens). For every `ValOrDefDef` with a declared result type and a non-empty rhs, for every token of the rhs, apply one mutation (a digit added to a literal, the token deleted, an identifier replaced by a sibling's name, `???` inserted, the rhs wrapped in a block) through the session (`run` on the new content, as Metals sends it). Read `myLastEdit`: if it says `body edit of … (typed|skipped)`, compare against a fresh `InteractiveDriver` on the new content: a hover at every identifier (innermost tree class, span, type, symbol's owner path and definition span, as `SessionEditSuite` does), the diagnostics of the completed session as a multiset (level, span, message), `children` of every sealed class defined in the file (`sym.children` by name), and the identity of every member symbol of the file before and after (`eq`), which checks the re-index refined rather than replaced. Then revert the edit and check the session equals the original's answers (an edit and its inverse compose to a layout edit). Metrics: the fraction kept, the refusal reasons' histogram, the poison count (should be zero; a poison is a classifier gap).

**Mutation test, one operator per catalogue row.** Each inserts a feature into a body (or beside it) and asserts the disjunction "the classifier refused (fresh run, reason logged)" or "the kept session's answers equal the fresh run's", recording which branch: (a) drop the result type; (b) prefix `inline`/`transparent inline`; (c) insert `class Local extends S` (S sealed in the file) into the OLD body, and separately into the NEW body with a sibling that summons `Mirror.SumOf[S]` or matches on S exhaustively, in both member orders; (d) insert a local `given Show[Int]` and a sibling summoning `Show[Int]`; (e) a macro splice; (f) change a default argument, add one, remove one; (g) edit an export/import/parent in the enclosing template; (h) add an overload sibling and edit the body of one alternative while a third member calls the overloaded name; (i) change an annotation argument; a `final val` used as an annotation argument; (j) add/remove `@nowarn` in the body, an error from a `transparent inline` sibling inlined into D, a "Conflicting definitions" at D; (k) delete the rhs entirely in a class, an object and a trait; (l) edit inside an extension method, a pattern definition, an opaque type's rhs. The toy predicts the branch for each row (table above); the test turns the prediction into a check and the x-ray's reason log into a measurement of how often each exclusion fires on real files.

## 6. The Java toy: roots, the victim rule, the abort with a waiter, root scratch

### What was added

`ConcurrentKernel` has three new options and `ConcurrentHarness` a third dimension.

- `RootMode { FACT, PAIR, FILE }`: what a completion claims and the other reader waits on. `FACT` is the Namer's root, the symbol. `PAIR` is the symbol loader's: a class, its companion and every member of either are one root `pair <Name>` (the toy puts the members under the pair because the toy's class completion forces nothing, so a lock order needs the members' completions to hold it; in dotc the classfile's members are read under the loader's completion too). `FILE` is TASTy's: `file <Name>`. The claim is reentrant for its owner (`Claim.depth`); the black hole stays per symbol (`Sched.holes`), so a re-entry on the owner is still the cycle of today. The wait-for check (`closesCycle`) follows owners of claims, 10.7's `wouldDeadlock`.
- `CycleRule { RAISE, VICTIM }`: what the reader that closes a wait-for cycle does. `RAISE` throws `CyclicReference` (§4.2 as written, the previous kernel). `VICTIM` is step 4's deviation: a reader at `namerDepth == 0` throws `Victim`, unwinds, releases everything, gives the turn back once (the driver re-queues the request) and runs `serve` again, at most 5 times, then answers `<livelock>`; a reader inside a Namer completion is never aborted: if the other party can be, it is woken with `abortPending`; if both are inside Namer completions the lower reader id raises, the other being woken with `raisePending`. The depth counts completions under `FACT` roots only: a loader's or a TASTy completion can run again, so a reader inside one is a body-typing reader for the rule.
- `scratchFinally`: per `FILE` root, `Sched.reading` is TASTy's `treeAtAddr` with its `PoisonTree`: a symbol is added when its completion starts and removed when it publishes; a second entry is an `UnpicklingError` ("cyclic reference while unpickling definition"), which the typer does not catch as a cycle, so the request that meets it fails (`<failed: ...>`), the harness's whole-unit typing included. With `scratchFinally` false the removal is only on the publishing path, as `TreeUnpickler.readIndexedDef` (lines 953-965) has it; true adds it to the `finally`.
- Harness: `// cancel: A` in a scenario runs, for every pair `(i, j)`, the first reader cancelled at each of its safepoints `c` (key `i,j@c`, `Reader.cancelAt`); its own answer is `<cancelled>` and not compared, the second reader's and the units' must be the reference's. A divergence is a *false cycle* when every differing answer carries a cyclic reference the reference does not have (I54, I87); the table gained the columns false cycle, aborts (victim aborts summed over the pairs) and livelock (pairs where a reader hit the bound).

### Claims this makes about dotc

1. `CompletionRoots.acquire` raising `CyclicReference` (`cyclicWait`) at a wait-for cycle through a coarse root (`SymbolLoaders`' class+module root, `TreeUnpickler.root` per file) reports a cycle no sequential order has whenever two readers take two roots in opposite orders without a dependency between the facts: I54 and lesson 6 reproduced in 7 of 48 pairs, with the oracle showing none.
2. `Reader.namerCompletionDepth == 0` as the victim test is right for loader and TASTy completions: aborting the closing reader and retrying its request gives the oracle's answer in every pair of both lock-order scenarios, with one abort per affected pair and no livelock, provided the retry is re-queued through `InteractiveDriver` rather than re-run in place (re-run in place, the same cycle re-forms up to the bound).
3. For Namer roots the victim rule cannot help, because a Namer root is held only from inside a Namer completion, so both parties of a wait-for cycle between Namer roots are at depth > 0 and the rule's only branch is "one raises": `CompletionRoots`' residue on `Settings.Setting` (I74's victim selection, I68) is the root-pair scenario one level down, and the toy says the fix is to tell a lock order from a dependency (does the waited-on completion's rule ask for the waiter's symbol? I68's next step) or re-runnable Namer completions (I8), not a different victim.
4. `TreeUnpickler.readIndexedDef` leaving its `PoisonTree` in `treeAtAddr` on an unwind (no try/finally) is a second source of "Cyclic reference while unpickling" under any abort of a reader inside a TASTy completion, a cancellation or a victim abort: the next reader of that file's root, the aborted reader's own retry, and the whole-file typing all fail on it (573 of 900 and 203 of 336 cancel runs, 7 of 48 victim pairs); a `finally` that removes the entry, or `root.owned`'s unwind path clearing the file's poisoned addresses, brings every count to 0. This is the candidate for I87's unfound second source (`UnpicklingError ... address 350 in unit /Soak.scala`).
5. `onUnwind` resetting `Touched` and releasing the root in the owner's unwind path (I33) is what makes the waiter's takeover sound: with the hole released, cancelling the owner at every safepoint of every pair leaves the waiter's answer and the whole-file answer the oracle's on every Namer-root kernel (0 races in 900 runs).
