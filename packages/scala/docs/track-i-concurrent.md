# Track I — Two readers over one session: the model

Step 1 of the multi-reader plan (the scala3 checkout's `docs/_docs/internals/interactive-multi-reader.md`, §7 and §8), 23 October 2026: the four statements of §7 in Lean, over `Query.Lazy`'s store, and the concurrent kernel and scheduler in the Java toy. [track-i.md](track-i.md) is the single-reader story this extends; its §7 (`Query/Interrupt.lean`) and §12 are what the statements here are the concurrent counterparts of.

## Summary for a busy reader

Two requests at once on one dotc session is two evaluators over one lazy store. The design proposes K2's lazy resolve: a completion in progress has an owner, every other reader waits on its barrier, and an unwound completion leaves the symbol completable. The model says what that buys and where it stops:

- **Linearizability holds for every interleaving, and needs only a fixed point.** [`run_agree`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L467): with black holes tagged by owner and a barrier, under every schedule of force steps and aborts, every value either forcer stores or answers is the fixed point's, the value a sequential `forceAll` of the two demand lists gives. The argument is [`forceA_agree`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L158)'s invariant read over two stacks: a frame is the rest of its rule after questions answered with published values, and only the owner sees a rule in progress. Progress (that both forcers finish) is checked by `decide` over every schedule on the scenarios.
- **A wait-for cycle is a dependency cycle.** [`run_ranked_no_cyc`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L815): under a ranked fixed point no schedule reports a cycle, self or cross-thread, because a forcer's stack ranks strictly downward and a hole is owned only while its fact is on its owner's stack; a wait that would close a wait-for cycle would rank each forcer's question below the other's. So `CyclicReference` in the reader that closes the cycle (§4.2 of the design) reports a cycle of the rules, as the sequential evaluator's does ([`cycle_unranked`](../../query/Query/Lazy.lean#L176)). Which reader reports it, and at which fact, depends on the schedule, as sequentially it depends on the order of demands ([`cross_cycle_report_site_varies`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L920)); the toy shows the same, in 15 of 81 interleavings of the cross-thread cycle scenario, and never deadlocks.
- **A peek at the other reader's hole is a race; a peek at one's own is not.** [`peek_any_races`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L943): two schedules store two different values of one fact, under rules that are ranked, where the sequential evaluator never peeks `absent` ([`forcePeek_ok`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L467)). [`peek_own_agrees`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L950): with `absent` only for the forcer's own holes and a wait on the other's, every schedule gives the sequential answer. In the toy, `findImplicit` skipping a view whose member is being completed races in 24 of 63 interleavings when "being completed" means by either reader, and in none when it means by this one. The thirteen `isCompleting` sites must mean "on this reader".
- **An aborted owner's waiter takes over, if the hole is released.** [`abort_waiter_takes_over`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L971): aborting the owner after any prefix of any schedule, the waiter answers as a run without the owner would, with the owner's finished work kept ([`abort_restart_ok`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L208) with a waiter). [`abort_keep_hole_waiter_stuck`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L983): with dotc's discipline, `Touched` set and never reset, the hole is owned by a reader that no longer exists and the waiter waits for the rest of the run. Sequentially that discipline made an aborted fact a cyclic reference ([`touched_stays_cyclic`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L287)); concurrently it is a barrier nobody releases. Step 4 of the plan has to reset `Touched` on unwinding, which `healLeakedTouched` does after the fact today.

Nothing failed. The one statement the method sharpened is the first: the design asked for agreement with a sequential `forceAll` as a property of ranked rules, and it holds for any fixed point, ranking being needed only for the absence of cycles, as it was for [`forceA_agree`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L158) against [`abort_restart_ok`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L208).

## 1. The machine

`Query/Concurrent.lean`, over `Query.Lazy` and `Query.Interrupt`; no Mathlib, no `native_decide`.

A state (`CSt`) is the store of finished facts, a hole per fact in progress with its owner (`holes : Q → Option Who`), and two forcers (`Forcer`), each a list of demands (the head is the one in progress), a stack of frames (`Frame`: the fact being forced and the rest of its rule, stopped at its next question or at its value) and the answers so far. A scheduler step (`step`) runs one forcer by one force step: read a finished fact, enter a rule (take the hole, push a frame), continue a rule with a published value, publish a value (store it, release the hole, pop), wait on the other forcer's hole, or unwind on a cycle (`unwind`: frames dropped, holes released, the demand answered `cyc`). A frame never hands its value to the frame below directly: it publishes, and the frame below reads the store at its next step, so there is one way to learn a value and it is through the store. A rule's intermediate state is seen by its owner only; a re-entry on the owner is a cycle (`want`), as today. The wait-for cycle check is `closesCycle`: each forcer waits on at most one fact, so following the owner of the hole back to the waiting forcer is the whole check, Salsa's cross-thread cycle detection in two lines. An `Act` is a step of one forcer or its abort; `runActs` runs a list of them; `runSched` runs a schedule prefix and then a fair round-robin tail; `scheds n` is every prefix of `n` steps.

Rules are [`PRules`](../../query/Query/Lazy.lean#L231) (they may peek) so that one machine serves §4; [`NoPeek`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L247) is the hypothesis that a rule set only forces. A peek at a fact in progress answers `absent` when the hole's owner is one [`PeekMode`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L61) lets it see: `own`, this forcer's; `any`, either's.

## 2. The four statements

| claim | theorem | by |
|---|---|---|
| **Linearizability.** Under every schedule of steps and aborts, with either peek mode and either unwinding discipline, the store agrees with the fixed point and every answer given is the fixed point's | [`run_agree`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L467), from the step invariant [`step_inv`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L371) (`Inv`: the store agrees, every frame is the rest of its rule under the fixed point by `Reach`, every answer is the fixed point's) | induction on the schedule; a fixed point of force-only rules, no ranking |
| Both forcers finish, with the sequential answers and store: the chain forced from both ends, and [`nested_inside_frame_cycles`](../../query/Query/Interrupt.lean#L415)'s request on its own forcer, where the cycle the same-thread interleaving made is a wait that succeeds | [`chain_linearizable`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L865), [`nested3_linearizable`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L876): all 64 schedules of six steps and a fair tail | `decide` |
| **Cycles.** Under a ranked fixed point no schedule reports a cycle, self or cross-thread | [`run_ranked_no_cyc`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L815), from [`step_rinv`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L625) ([`RInv`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L510): stacks rank strictly downward, every question ranks below its frame, a hole's owner has its fact on its stack) | induction; a ranked fixed point, holes released on unwinding |
| so a cycle reported under some schedule means the rules have no ranked fixed point, and `CyclicReference` in the forcer that closes a wait-for cycle is sound | [`waitCycle_unranked`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L823) | contrapositive |
| The two signatures that need each other, forced from two forcers: both report a cycle, neither deadlocks, nothing on the cycle is stored, the well-founded neighbour is; the sequential run reports both too; which fact is named depends on the schedule | [`cross_cycle_both_report`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L901), [`cross_cycle_sequential`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L911), [`cross_cycle_report_site_varies`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L920) | `decide` |
| **Peek.** A peek at the other forcer's hole: two schedules, two stored values of one fact, under ranked rules | [`peek_any_races`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L943) | `decide` |
| A peek at one's own holes only, waiting on the other's: every schedule the sequential answer | [`peek_own_agrees`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L950) | `decide` |
| **Abort with a waiter.** The owner aborted after any prefix of any schedule of up to six steps: the waiter answers as a run without the owner, and the store holds the chain | [`abort_waiter_takes_over`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L971) (`abortScheds`, 127 schedules) | `decide` |
| With the hole left set on unwinding, the waiter waits for the rest of the run and the request is never answered | [`abort_keep_hole_waiter_stuck`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L983) | `decide` |

What the theorems leave to `decide` is progress: [`run_agree`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L467) says nothing stored is wrong, not that anything is stored. A general progress theorem needs a measure over both stacks and the schedule's fairness, and the scenarios say what it would have to cover (a waiter wakes when its hole is published or released; a forcer that reports a cycle unwinds to its next demand).

## 3. The toy

[`ConcurrentKernel.java`](../probes/interrupt/src/pctoy/ConcurrentKernel.java) is the path session (`dotc-session-path`, every fix on) with two readers: one run over the loaded sources, each request a thread that types the declaration containing its target. `force` has the owner on the black hole: a completer in progress is entered by nobody else, a re-entry by its owner is a `CyclicReference`, another reader waits on it and takes it over if the owner unwinds without publishing. A wait that would close a wait-for cycle (the owner is waiting on a fact this reader holds) is a `CyclicReference` here. A declaration's typing is a fact with a barrier too (§3.2 of the design): a reader whose target is in a declaration the other is typing waits. `findImplicit` is the peek: a view whose member is being completed is skipped, as scalac's `isCyclicOrErroneous` skips a `LOCKED` candidate, with "being completed" meaning by either reader (`dotc-concurrent-peek-any`) or by this one (`dotc-concurrent`). The request's `targetPath`, the one piece of per-request scratch `Global` keeps in a field, is saved and restored at each switch (`dotc-concurrent-shared-scratch` does not).

The readers are threads, but only one runs at a time: every safepoint hands control to the harness through a semaphore pair, so an interleaving is a function of the schedule. [`ConcurrentHarness.java`](../probes/interrupt/src/pctoy/ConcurrentHarness.java) runs every pair `(i, j)`: the first reader alone to its `i`th safepoint, the second to its `j`th, then alternating a safepoint at a time, a waiting reader skipped; the harness then types the rest of each unit. The reference is the first reader run to its answer before the second starts, which is also checked against `dotc-session-path` replaying the two requests. A divergence whose every differing answer is a cyclic reference on both sides is the cycle reported at another site; any other is a race.

[`results-concurrent.txt`](../probes/interrupt/results-concurrent.txt), from [`run.sh`](../probes/interrupt/run.sh) `--concurrent`:

| scenario | pairs | `dotc-concurrent` | `dotc-concurrent-peek-any` | `dotc-concurrent-shared-scratch` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`concurrent-cross-cycle`](../probes/interrupt/scenarios/concurrent-cross-cycle.toy) (A.x needs B.y, B.y needs A.x, each with a safepoint before the cross reference) | 81 | 0 races, cycle at the other site in 15, no deadlock | same | same |
| [`concurrent-peek-implicit`](../probes/interrupt/scenarios/concurrent-peek-implicit.toy) (Use.a resolves `t.foo` through the view `Ops` while the other reader is inside `Ops.foo`) | 63 | 0 | **24 races**: `value foo is not a member of T` | 0 |
| `concurrent-shared-facts` (two declarations both needing `Lib.v`) | 110 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `concurrent-same-decl` (two targets in one declaration) | 121 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `concurrent-block-scratch` (two targets inside blocks of two declarations) | 64 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The last row is a negative result: sharing `targetPath` between the readers changes which statements a block types when, but not the answers, because the statements it skips are resumed when the unit is finished. The toy has no other kind-A state (no comparers, no context pools, no typer state), so kind A is the design table's claim, not the toy's finding.

## 4. What this says for dotc

Three things for steps 3 and 4 of the plan.

**The barrier is the whole correctness argument.** [`run_agree`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L467) needs no ranking and no fairness: a reader that only ever reads published values, and publishes only once and only when consistent, cannot store a wrong value whatever the interleaving. Everything else (cycles, peeks, aborts) is about progress and about where an error is reported. That is the order in which to build it: the barrier and the owner first, under one coarse lock (step 3), then the finer points.

**`Touched` must be reset by the unwinding reader, not healed later.** [`abort_keep_hole_waiter_stuck`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L983) is the session's `healLeakedTouched` seen from a waiter: today a leaked `Touched` is healed when the next request on the same thread meets it; with a waiter there is no next request, only a reader blocked on a barrier whose owner is gone. The release belongs in the unwinding path (a `finally` in `completeFrom`, scalac's discipline), and `Run.completing` becomes the owner field the barrier reads.

**`isCompleting` on another reader is "wait", never "skip".** [`peek_any_races`](../../query/Query/Concurrent.lean#L943) is the statement; the toy's `findImplicit` is the first of the thirteen sites made concrete, and the oracle suite with two readers will find the others by the same signature: an answer that differs from the single-reader answer and contains no cyclic reference.
