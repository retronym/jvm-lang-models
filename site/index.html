What can this model do for me?

Lean models of the tools that build JVM languages: the JVM linker, javac, scalac and dotc, incremental compilation, Zinc, MiMa and the IntelliJ Scala plugin. Each layer answers one question, is calibrated against the real tool, and only then has theorems built on it. Pick who you are on the right; click a layer to see what it holds. The literate pages read the models next to the JVMS, JLS and SLS clauses they formalise; the API documentation lists every definition and theorem.

What it will not do for you

  • It does not verify scalac or Zinc. The models are calibrated against them; the theorems are about the models, and the Zinc model's hypotheses are the specification a bridge must meet.
  • Hashing is assumed perfect. Collisions of Zinc's 32-bit name hashes are excluded by the abstraction obligation.
  • Incremental = clean (T3) needs acyclic dependencies or annotated interfaces. A mix of annotated and inferred members in a cycle is unproved.
  • Precision is per instance. The framework proves a floor (necessary_invalidated); "invalidates no more than needed" is not a framework theorem.
  • The set of units is fixed. Added and deleted sources are modelled as absent sources.
  • Checks are bounded. native_decide results are examples over a space, never theorems. MiMa's corrected rules are sound on the edit space, not yet for every library.
