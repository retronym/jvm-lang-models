# Using the models against the specifications: soundness, omissions, contradictions

12 October 2026, a plan for review. The models were built to answer build-tool questions. The same models, each rule citing the clause it implements and each layer calibrated against the implementation, can be turned on the specifications themselves.

## Three kinds of question

**Soundness.** A static promise kept, or not, by the dynamic semantics. Not type soundness in the DOT sense first, but the promises an implementation makes across its own phases and across the platform: refchecks accepts a class, so no call on it throws `AbstractMethodError`; a match is exhaustive, so it never throws `MatchError`; a `val` is initialised before it is read. The repository has already found one such failure (scalac's trait setter dropped under re-abstraction, [scala/bug#13199](https://github.com/scala/bug/issues/13199), an `AbstractMethodError` after refchecks accepted the class) and has the layers to state the property: `Members` for the static side, `Lower` for the encoding, `Jvm` for the dynamic side.

**Omissions.** A rule the model needs that no clause states. Each calibration has produced some: the SLS says nothing about which of two re-abstraction behaviours is meant, the JVMS gives the linking exceptions but not their order (the model has HotSpot's order for sealed, final and access checks, and for a constructor's access check before its declared-class check), the JLS says when an edit is an error but not what happens otherwise ([`BinaryCompat.lean`](../../../literate/Literate/BinaryCompat.lean) already says so). These are recorded in prose today, scattered through the track documents.

**Contradictions.** Two sources that disagree on a program: a clause against its own example (the JVMS §5.4.5 example disagreed with a model built from the clause's prose, which is how transitive overriding was found), a clause against another clause (SLS §5.1.4 "a concrete definition always overrides an abstract one" against the compilers' re-abstraction, documented in [`docs/upstream`](../../upstream/README.md)), one specification against another at a layer boundary (JLS §8.4.8 overriding against JVMS §5.4.5 overriding; JLS chapter 13's compatibility claims against what the JVMS makes link), or a specification against the implementation it describes where the implementation is right (JDK-8356942 is the other way round).

## Why these models can answer them

- Every rule in the JVM, Java and Scala layers cites a clause, and the literate pages quote the clause verbatim with a CI check that the quotation is exact. The citation is already the unit of evidence; what is missing is the inverse index.
- Verdicts on bounded spaces are decided by the kernel, so "clause A and clause B disagree on this program" is a theorem about the encodings, not an opinion about the prose.
- The probes, and now the compile server, decide which side an implementation takes, per program, at a few milliseconds each.
- The specification's own examples are programs. Encoding each one as a catalogue case checks the model, the implementation and the prose against each other at once; it found a bug the first time it was tried.

## Method

**A specification ledger, generated.** Each rule carries one of three tags: `cites` a clause, `unspecified` with the implementation evidence (which tool, which probe run), or `contradicts` with the two sources and the witness program. `model_stats.py` already generates a page from the sources; a sibling script generates `docs/specification-ledger.md`: per specification and section, the rules that cite it, the rules it fails to cover, and the contradictions, each linked to the Lean definition and the witness. The literate pages gain an "unspecified" and a "contradiction" callout drawn from the same tags. This is the deliverable that makes the scattered prose findings one list, and it is cheap: the tags are docstring conventions plus a script.

**Clause-versus-clause by decision.** Where two readings exist, encode both as predicates over the same space and have the kernel decide where they differ. The re-abstraction question is the template: SLS §5.1.4 literal, scalac, dotc and javac are four predicates over the 4,704-hierarchy space, and the programs where they differ are the content of the discussion draft. The same shape fits JLS §8.4.8 against JVMS §5.4.5 over the chain space, and the JLS §13.4 claims against `Jvm` linking over the Java catalogue.

**Examples as cases.** Every worked example in JVMS chapter 5, JLS chapter 13 and SLS chapter 5 becomes a catalogue case, decided by the kernel and run by the probe. A pass is a quotation-checked, kernel-decided reading of the example; a fail is a finding in one of the three places.

**Soundness as a theorem across layers.** State each promise as: static verdict on the source implies the dynamic outcome on the lowered classfiles, over `Members`, `Lower` and `Jvm`. Prove it on the bounded space by kernel decision first, with the known counterexamples excluded by name, then for every program once the three layers share one Scala semantics (the port of the Zinc toys, in the roadmap). The counterexamples are the findings; the theorem is the certificate that there are no others of that shape.

## Questions, in order of readiness

1. **Refchecks accepted, so no `AbstractMethodError`.** The property behind [scala/bug#13199](https://github.com/scala/bug/issues/13199). Statement: if `Members` accepts `C` under a dialect and selects owner `O` for `m`, then `Lower C` linked by `Jvm` runs `O`'s lowered method (or its forwarder). Everything needed exists; the probe's runtime check already observes the right-hand side. Expected: the trait-setter case on 2.12 and 2.13, and possibly re-abstraction cases on Scala 3, as the only failures on the 4,704-program space. Deliverable: the theorem on the space, the counterexamples as named exclusions, the spec ledger entries.
2. **Re-abstraction, settled.** The four predicates and their disagreement set, then the SLS wording that matches the chosen rule, as the upstream draft proposes. Mostly done; needs the ledger and the decision.
3. **JLS chapter 13 audited clause by clause.** Each "binary compatible" or "not binary compatible" sentence of §13.4 and §13.5 as an example over the Java catalogue and `Jvm`. Where the JLS says compatible and the JVMS makes it fail to link, or the reverse, that is a contradiction between the two specifications; where the JLS is silent (the "otherwise" cases), an omission. [`Jls13.lean`](../../../packages/java/Java/Jls13.lean) has 20 examples; the chapter has about sixty claims.
4. **JVMS ordering of linkage checks.** The model has HotSpot's order where the JVMS gives none. List them from the ledger and check whether JVMS §5.3.5 and §5.4.3 intend an order; each is either a clarification request on the JVMS or a note that the order is implementation-defined and observable. Small, and a direct product of the J1 to J6 calibrations.
5. **Overriding across the JLS and JVMS.** JLS §8.4.8.1 and JVMS §5.4.5 define overriding differently (the JVM's is transitive and package-sensitive in its own way). Over the chain space and a J7 default-method space, decide where javac's notion and the JVM's notion disagree, and whether javac's generated code is affected. Prior finding: none yet; the shape is where the J6 bugs lived.
6. **Exhaustivity under library evolution.** A sealed hierarchy gains a case in the library; the client's exhaustive match was compiled against the old one and throws `MatchError` with no recompilation and no MiMa report. Soundness under evolution, at the boundary of three tools. Needs a match-site in the Scala source model (small) and the sealed support `Jvm` already has.
7. **Initialisation.** With Track J4's bodies and a `<clinit>`/constructor order on top, state dotc's safe-init promise (an accepted class never reads a field before its initialiser ran) and calibrate against `-Ysafe-init` and HotSpot. The counterexamples are the init checker's known gaps.
8. **Type soundness, by oracle.** The DOT line of work proves a core calculus sound; the question for an implementation is the gap between the calculus and scalac. The repository's method applies: enumerate programs over path-dependent and projected types in the `ScalaTypes` language, compile with scalac and dotc through the server, run them, and treat a `ClassCastException` with no cast in the source as an unsoundness. Amin and Tate's 2016 counterexamples are the first catalogue cases. A mountain, after the type language grows to include the shapes those counterexamples need.

## What it costs, and the order

Items 1, 2 and 4 are weeks of a session each, on existing layers. The ledger is a day and should come first, since every later item writes into it. Item 3 is mechanical and large. Items 5 and 6 each need a small model extension. Items 7 and 8 are mountains and sit behind Track J4 and the type-language growth.

Upstream, each finding has a venue: SLS wording (scala/scala3 under `docs/_spec`), JLS and JVMS clarifications (OpenJDK's spec bug component, as JDK-8356942 was a conformance bug), compiler issues where the implementation is wrong. The [`docs/upstream`](../../upstream/README.md) table is the queue.
